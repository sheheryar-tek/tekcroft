  1. We shape the roadmap first, as product and user flows. From there, our designers turn those requirements into wireframes and prototypes you can react to before development begins.

  2. When the product calls for it, we build natively in Swift or Kotlin. For products where getting to both platforms quickly matters more, Flutter and React Native give us another route.

  3. Behind the app, we handle the less visible pieces too: backend architecture, databases, APIs, payment systems, CRMs, ERPs, analytics integrations, etc.

  4. Testing happens on real devices as well as through automated checks. Once everything is ready, we handle the App Store and Google Play submission process.

  5. Launch isn’t the finish line for us. OS updates, security patches, monitoring, or further feature work can all stay within the same team.

Why choose us

What Tekcroft Does Differently for Apps?

Transparent, Milestone-Based Pricing

You pay for what got built this month, not a large deposit against a delivery date six months out. You see working software before you fund the next phase.

Real Compliance Experience, Not Just Badges

On real Tekcroft projects we have implemented HIPAA for healthcare and telehealth apps handling patient data, PCI-DSS for fintech and payment-processing apps, GDPR for consumer apps serving EU users with personal data, and SOC 2 controls for enterprise apps that require audited security practices, not badges on a marketing page, but controls built into live products.

A Process Built to Prevent Scope Creep

Almost 60% of app development projects go over their initial budget, almost always because scope wasn’t locked down before development started. We scope in writing, screen by screen, before a single sprint begins, so every change request is a conversation, not a surprise invoice.

We Don’t Disappear After Launch

The same team that built your app stays on after it ships. Launch weeks include crash and performance monitoring with fixes shipped fast. Every month we handle OS updates, security patches, and dependency upgrades. When you’re ready for version two, the people who know the codebase build it. Typical post-launch support runs on a monthly retainer with agreed response windows for critical issues during business hours.
