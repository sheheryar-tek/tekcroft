Buyer’s guide

How to Choose a Web Design Company

A bad web project usually goes wrong for the same handful of reasons: an agency won’t put things in writing, or disappears once the deposit clears. It can happen at a boutique or a larger website development agency. Here’s exactly what you should get committed to, upfront:

  1. Who owns the website?

    Make sure the domain, hosting account, source code and other key assets are registered in your name.

  2. How are payments structured?

    Milestone-based payments give both sides clear checkpoints throughout the project.

  3. What’s included in the quoted price?

    Ask which design, development, revisions, integrations, testing or support are included before signing.

  4. What’s the timeline?

    A written timeline gives you a clear expectation for each stage of the project.

  5. Can you see relevant work?

    Look beyond a general portfolio. Ask for examples close to your industry, website type or project requirements.

Work with a partner you can trust. Clear communication. Written agreements. Results that speak for themselves.
