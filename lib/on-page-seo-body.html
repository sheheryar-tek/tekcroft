Search Isn’t Just Google Anymore

Build Pages That Show Up in Search Results and AI Answers.

Search behavior is shifting rapidly; AI Overviews now appear on roughly 48% of tracked U.S. queries. These AI Overviews cause almost a 60% drop in the click-through rate of organic results, as compared to searches with no AI Overview. But it does not mean you should give up on rankings, just that you need to compete for a different type of visibility.

Brands that get cited in AI Overviews see 35% more organic clicks and 91% more paid clicks than brands that are not cited on the same query. Also, traffic from AI referrals converts at 4.4× the rate of standard organic traffic.

The catch here is that ranking in organic SERP results is not a guarantee of AI citation. Independent analysis found the share of AI Overview citations coming from pages that also rank in the organic top 10 fell from 76% to 38% in about eighteen months. Rankings and AI citations are becoming two separate battles, so treat them as two separate (but connected) deliverables.

On-page SEO is optimizing elements directly on a webpage: titles, headers, content, internal links, images, structured data. It helps search engines and AI systems understand and rank the page. On-page is different from off-page SEO (backlinks, brand mentions) and technical SEO (site-wide crawlability and indexation), though the three work together.

Cost increases with scope; a single-page audit and rewrite costs less than a full-site optimization project. (add Tekcroft’s actual pricing framework)

On-page SEO covers everything you control directly on your own pages. Off-page SEO covers signals earned elsewhere, like backlinks, brand mentions, or reviews. Websites need both, but on-page is the part you can improve immediately without waiting on outside factors.

SEO is not dead, just the definition of “visible” has split into two categories. About 68% of U.S. Google searches now end without a click (SparkToro, early 2026), largely due to AI Overviews and other zero-click features. That doesn’t mean search traffic is gone; it means there are now two ways to win: ranking in classic organic results, or being the source an AI Overview cites. On-page SEO, like a clean structure, clear entities, and schema markup, is a deciding factor in whether you get cited or skipped.

No single tool can tell you what to fix first. Agencies like us use crawlers such as Screaming Frog and Sitebulb to find technical issues, then use Search Console and content tools to understand what those issues mean for actual search performance.

Typically, you can see early movement within 3-6 months, with significant results showing up between 6 and 12 months, as per former Google spokesperson Maile Ohye (cited by Search Engine Land). Sometimes websites see a faster initial jump by fixing some obvious issues like missing metadata or broken internal linking. Google’s John Mueller has described this as a “nice big jump” (cited by Search Engine Journal) that tends to level off into steadier, longer-term growth after the easy wins are gone.

Tekcroft works with companies of every size, with the same dedication and effort. The only change is the order of priority for fixes, based on size and competition. Whether you are a five-page local business or a multi-thousand-page ecommerce catalogue, the on-page optimization framework remains the same.

Yes, on-page SEO differs across CMSes to some extent. As every platform has its own restrictions, like Shopify’s URL structure, Squarespace’s limited metadata controls, WordPress plugin conflicts, etc., all of these require an implementation that works with them. That’s why the strategy remains the same; only the execution adapts to the CMS.
