Our AI Chatbot Development Process

Our chatbot developers follow a five-stage development process designed for accountability, not guesswork. This is where most chatbot app development projects succeed or fail.

The 5-stage process 0102030405 of five stages

01Discovery and Requirements Mapping

We map your systems, workflows, and edge cases before starting the development process. This helps avoid scope changes that usually ruin most projects.

02Conversation Design and Escalation Logic

Our dialogue flow, memory stack, fallback system, and escalation triggers all work as a single unit. 81% of customers want service to flow continuously without any need for repetition. Full context is transferred once your chatbot completes the conversation.

03Development, RAG Setup and Model Integration

We develop the chatbot, connect to your knowledge base, and connect to your selected model. Integration is done with testing on real data rather than scripts.

04Testing, QA and Deployment

We test against real conversations, edge cases, and adversarial prompts before going live. Monitoring begins with the first real conversation, not afterwards.

05Ongoing Monitoring, Retraining and Maintenance

We measure performance and keep retraining your chatbot according to any changes within your business or data. This alone solves most issues around accuracy.

Buyer’s guide

How to Choose a Web Design Company

A bad web project usually goes wrong for the same handful of reasons: an agency won’t put things in writing, or disappears once the deposit clears. It can happen at a boutique or a larger website development agency. Here’s exactly what you should get committed to, upfront:

  1. Who owns the website?

    Make sure the domain, hosting account, source code and other key assets are registered in your name.

  2. How are payments structured?

    Milestone-based payments give both sides clear checkpoints throughout the project.

  3. What’s included in the quoted price?

    Ask which design, development, revisions, integrations, testing or support are included before signing.

  4. What’s the timeline?

    A written timeline gives you a clear expectation for each stage of the project.

  5. Can you see relevant work?

    Look beyond a general portfolio. Ask for examples close to your industry, website type or project requirements.

Work with a partner you can trust. Clear communication. Written agreements. Results that speak for themselves.

Milestone-based, and in writing before work starts. The scope is agreed screen by screen, then you pay per milestone: on prototype sign-off, for each build sprint as it ships, and at launch — not as a large deposit against a date six months out. Typical ranges run $10,000–$30,000 for a simple MVP, $30,000–$80,000 for a mid-complexity app, and $80,000–$400,000+ for enterprise-grade work.

The single most useful thing you can bring us is a screen-by-screen breakdown of what your app needs to do, written as if you had to explain it to someone with zero context. It needs to be specific, because it makes the quote you get comparable.

We wouldn’t recommend building two native apps just because you eventually want to be on both stores. An MVP is often the better starting point. It puts the core product in users’ hands sooner and gives you evidence about what people actually use before you spend on the full feature set.

The same team stays on. That means crash and performance monitoring from launch week, OS updates and security patches every month, and feature work when you’re ready for version two. Budget for it: annual maintenance typically runs 15–20% of the original build cost.
