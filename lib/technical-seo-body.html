Warning signs

Signs Your Website Has a Technical SEO Problem

Here’s a simple check you can do yourself that’ll tell you right away if your website has a technical SEO problem:

  • 01

    Your website traffic dropped, but you have no idea why. The common cause is a crawl, indexing, or Web Vitals issue.

  • 02

    You published and interlinked the page, but Google still won’t index it. The issue can be a canonical conflict, a noindex tag left in place, or a crawl budget issue pulling bots away from the important pages.

  • 03

    The site looks fine but takes time to load. Render-blocking scripts, unoptimized images, or bloated JavaScript are the culprit.

  • 04

    Ranking in Google, but not showing up in rich results or AI Overviews. That’s a structured data and schema gap, with an increasing technical readiness gap for AI search engines.

  • 05

    About to migrate or redesign, but terrified of losing rankings. A migration without a technical SEO plan is the single fastest way to lose months of organic growth overnight.

Technical SEO

Is Technical SEO Still Relevant in the Age of AI Search?

It is more important now. ChatGPT, Perplexity, or AI Overviews from Google will not magically understand your site. Most, if not all of them, will use a process referred to as retrieval-augmented generation. In short, the AI will actively pull live pages, read the code, extract facts, and then respond to queries. This means the same basic technical SEO that gets you crawled and ranked by Google will determine whether an AI engine can find you, read you, or even cite you.

AI crawlers hit many of the same walls that traditional search engines historically have. Googlebot can’t render your JS? Broken schema? Hidden pages that require 3 or more clicks to access? Any of these, and it means you’ll be missed by AI search technology.

What’s changed technically:

  • Clean crawl paths matter more.

    AI bots need clear site architecture and working sitemaps to navigate your pages- the same basics.

  • Schema does double duty.

    Structured data is how AI engines identify facts and context they can safely lift and cite, along with earning rich results.

  • Robots.txt now gates AI access too.

    Beyond Googlebot and Bingbot, your crawl directives need to deliberately allow (or block) AI-specific crawlers for your visibility strategy.

  • Site structure reads as a knowledge graph.

    A logical content hierarchy helps users navigate; it also helps AI systems understand how your pages relate to each other and to the topic as a whole.

Technical SEO is the foundation of every search engine or AI crawler you are going to interact with. Skipping it means you will remain invisible to both AI and search engines.
