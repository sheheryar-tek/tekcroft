Warning signs

Signs Your Website Has a Technical SEO Problem

Here’s a simple check you can do yourself that’ll tell you right away if your website has a technical SEO problem.

  • 01

    Your website traffic dropped, but you have no idea why. The common cause is a crawl, indexing, or Web Vitals issue — something that changed underneath the site rather than on it.

  • 02

    You published and interlinked the page, but Google still won’t index it. The issue can be a canonical conflict, a noindex tag left in place, or a crawl budget issue pulling bots away from the important pages.

  • 03

    The site looks fine but takes time to load. Render-blocking scripts, unoptimised images, or bloated JavaScript are the culprit.

  • 04

    Ranking in Google, but not showing up in rich results or AI Overviews. That is a structured data and schema gap, with an increasing technical readiness gap for AI search engines.

  • 05

    About to migrate or redesign, but terrified of losing rankings. A migration without a technical SEO plan is the single fastest way to lose months of organic growth overnight.

Technical SEO

Is Technical SEO Still Relevant in the Age of AI Search?

It is more important now. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google’s AI Overviews will not magically understand your site. Most of them work by retrieval-augmented generation: the model pulls live pages, reads the code, extracts the facts and only then answers. The same technical SEO that gets you crawled and ranked by Google is what decides whether an AI engine can find you, read you, or cite you.

AI crawlers hit the same walls traditional search engines always have. Googlebot cannot render your JavaScript. Schema is broken. A page is three clicks deep and nothing links to it. Any one of those and you are missed by AI search as well.

What has changed technically

  • Clean crawl paths matter more.

    AI bots need clear site architecture and working sitemaps to navigate your pages — the same basics.

  • Schema does double duty.

    Structured data is how AI engines identify facts and context they can safely lift and cite, along with earning rich results.

  • Robots.txt now gates AI access too.

    Beyond Googlebot and Bingbot, your crawl directives need to deliberately allow (or block) AI-specific crawlers for your visibility strategy.

  • Site structure reads as a knowledge graph.

    A logical content hierarchy helps users navigate; it also helps AI systems understand how your pages relate to each other and to the topic as a whole.

Technical SEO is the ground floor of every search engine and every AI crawler you will deal with. Skip it and you stay invisible to both.

Technical SEO makes your website readable to search engines, while on-page SEO optimizes the content and internal links on a given page. Technical and on-page SEO are both important, but if search engines cannot crawl or index your website, none of the content will ever be ranked.

Our technical SEO analysis checks crawlability, site structure, XML sitemaps and robots.txt, site architecture, canonical tags, redirects, page speed, Core Web Vitals, JavaScript, structured data, mobile-friendliness and more. This list is prioritized based on the severity of the issues.

Yes. The content quality doesn’t matter if search engines can’t crawl, render, or index a page — in other words, the page simply doesn’t exist for search engines.

Fixing crawlability, indexation, or site speed should be the top priority, since these issues determine whether any of the pages on your site are even able to rank. We categorize these problems on actual traffic data instead of assuming every problem is of equal importance.

Some improvements, like fixed crawl access, show up quickly. Ranking impact is linked to the severity of the issues the website had at the start and how often search engines recrawl the site. Typically, you’ll see an increase in rankings in two to four months.
