The ecommerce SEO problem

From Problems to Profits

Running an online store creates SEO problems a typical service website simply does not have — large catalogues, category structures, product variations and buyer intent all pulling in different directions.

The problem What holds your store back
What we do How we turn it into growth

Keyword Cannibalisation

Category, subcategory and product pages end up targeting the same commercial keywords, so Google cannot tell which one should rank.

Keyword Strategy & Mapping

We map intent-driven keywords to the right pages, remove the overlap and strengthen the authority of the ones that matter.

Thin or Duplicate Content

Writing unique descriptions for hundreds of products is hard. Repeated manufacturer copy gives search engines no reason to favour one page.

Unique, Optimised Content

We write product, category and supporting content that reads well and gives search engines a reason to rank it.

Products Buried in the Site

On large catalogues, products sit two or three levels from the homepage. Without a strong internal link structure they are never crawled, indexed or ranked.

Smart Site Architecture

We build a clean, logical structure with strong internal links, so every page worth finding gets found.

Traffic Without Sales

A page can rank well and still earn little. Someone researching a product needs different content from someone ready to buy.

Intent-Driven SEO That Converts

We target the right intent at every stage of the buyer journey, so the traffic you earn turns into revenue.

The real problem is not ranking more pages, but making them convert.

All of the above has to work together around how customers actually move from discovering a product to comparing it and finally buying it.

Ecommerce SEO optimizes online shopping channels. It involves optimizing technical structure, categories and products, content, and more. The goal is to attract relevant traffic that converts and results in ROI.

SEO services for ecommerce can cost anything between $1,500 and $15,000 a month in the US. It depends on the size of your catalog, the level of technical SEO required, website competition, content needed, and the agency’s experience. The type of contract — retainer, hourly, or one-time project — will also affect the final cost.

Regular SEO covers informational and brand awareness pages, whereas ecommerce SEO is for high-density product pages. More importantly, ecommerce SEO targets intent to purchase, as opposed to traffic amount.

Ecommerce SEO takes time to show results. Most online stores notice quick improvements in the technical and indexing aspects in the first few months. Ranking improvements, along with a noticeable increase in traffic, take three to six months, while significant, compounding effects take a minimum of six to twelve months.

All major store builders (Shopify, WooCommerce and others), open-source software such as Magento, and even custom builds can use our ecommerce SEO services to increase sales. Our WooCommerce SEO work addresses both the platform’s technical setup and the content and structure of your store.

SEO grows organic search, which is an owned rather than a rented channel — the traffic does not stop when a budget does. Shoppers who arrive through organic results tend to engage more deeply before buying, and unlike paid placements those listings keep working after the spend ends.

Avoid agencies that:

  • guarantee page-one rankings in a certain amount of time
  • do not provide real case studies and results from previous clients
  • offer prices that are too low for the work involved
  • cannot explain their strategy for your catalog and platform for the first 90 days

Category pages come first, since they are how you target the higher-volume phrases that lead to revenue from organic search. Product pages target specific, longer phrases that sit closer to the purchase. Most stores benefit from working on both in parallel, but category-page work often shows results faster.
